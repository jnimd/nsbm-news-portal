Session for NSBM Academic Staff to Embed Entrepreneurship Across the Curriculum
NSBM Green University conducted a Continuing Professional Development (CPD) session titled “Embedding Entrepreneurship Across the Curriculum: Practical Strategies for Teaching and Learning,” bringing together academic staff from all faculties to explore ways of integrating entrepreneurial thinking and practices into teaching and learning across disciplines.

The session was facilitated by Dr Adam Scott of Victoria University, Australia, who shared research-based insights and practical approaches drawn from nine instructional design principles developed in collaboration with Dr Nilusha Gallage, Lecturer and Coordinator – Undergraduate Courses at Victoria University. The session focused on translating these principles into actionable teaching strategies that academics could incorporate into their existing course units.

The discussions covered three key areas of curriculum design: content, teaching and assessment. Participants explored strategies for scaffolding learning materials to accommodate different levels of student readiness, using Generative AI to present complex theories through visual formats, and connecting academic concepts with real-world applications.

In relation to teaching approaches, the session highlighted the importance of balancing the use of Generative AI with interactive and discussion-based learning. Practical strategies included AI critique activities to strengthen students’ critical thinking, as well as collaborative team projects that encourage creativity, problem-solving and teamwork.

The assessment component focused on designing authentic learning experiences that reflect real-world entrepreneurial practices. These included venture pitches, reflective portfolios combining project work with personal insights, and progressive feedback mechanisms to support students throughout different stages of a project.

The session emphasised that entrepreneurship education should extend beyond traditional business disciplines. By embedding entrepreneurial thinking across the curriculum, universities can equip graduates from all academic backgrounds with the initiative, creativity and practical skills to identify opportunities, address challenges and create value in their respective fields.

NSBM extends its sincere appreciation to Dr Adam Scott and Dr Nilusha Gallage for sharing their research, expertise and valuable insights with the university’s academic community.

The session was organised by the Young Entrepreneurship Circle, in collaboration with the Department of Management and the Quality Assurance and Staff Development Center, as part of Global Entrepreneurship Week 2026.
